What kind of publication this is. The distinction that matters most for provenance is guideline versus not: a summary table or position paper restates recommendations made elsewhere, and a 1.9 product makes none of its own. Conflating them makes a citation resolve to the wrong authority. This case-sensitive code system http://smart.who.int/base/CodeSystem/KGPublicationType defines the following codes:

CodeDisplayDefinition
standard-guideline Standard guideline Recommendations on a specific topic or condition, usually new. Most WHO guidelines. Handbook §1.7.1.
consolidated-guideline Consolidated guideline Aggregates existing recommendations on a disease or condition, evaluated as up to date; may add new ones. Existing recommendations must be explicitly cross-referenced. Handbook §1.7.2.
interim-guideline Interim guideline Guidance when data are incomplete and more are expected; short shelf-life, states when an update is anticipated. Handbook §1.7.3.
rapid-advice-guideline Rapid advice guideline Produced in one to three months in a public health emergency, with a review-by date. Handbook §1.7.4, chapter 11.
emergency-guideline Emergency guideline Rapid response guidance within hours to days; may rest on expert opinion only. Handbook §1.7.4.
collaborative-guideline Collaborative guideline Developed with one or more external organizations sharing the remit. Handbook §1.8.1.
external-guideline External guideline Developed by an external organization and adopted by WHO. Handbook §1.8.2.
adapted-guideline Adapted guideline An existing WHO guideline adapted to a local context by a Member State. Handbook 1.8.3, 1.8.3,
position-paper Position paper A WHO position paper — for vaccines, the SAGE-reviewed statement of WHO's position published in the Weekly Epidemiological Record. States recommendations; the immunization summary tables are drawn from these.
summary-table Summary table A tabular restatement of recommendations made in other publications — e.g. "WHO recommendations for routine immunization – summary tables". Cite-worthy, but not the originating authority: a citation resolving here should normally be followed on to the recommendation it restates.
classification Classification A WHO classification, such as the Classification of Digital Health Interventions (CDHI). Its codes are cross-referenced through terminology-code, never modelled here.
implementation-guidance Implementation guidance An operational manual, implementation guide or tool based on approved guidelines — a "how to" document. Makes no recommendations of its own. Handbook §1.9. A DAK is one of these.
methodology Methodology A document describing how guidance is produced — the guideline development handbook itself. Handbook §1.9 (standard operating procedures).
supplement Supplement A web annex or supplementary document of another publication, typically holding systematic reviews, GRADE evidence profiles and evidence-to-decision tables. Handbook §12.1. Reached by hasSupplement.
